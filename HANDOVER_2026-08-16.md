## 1. Project objective and current outcome

K2 is a 12-DOF, 3D-printed biped driven by twelve Feetech STS3215 serial servos. The immediate progression is:

1. make hold and in-place marching reliable in MuJoCo and on the real robot;
2. make the MuJoCo model a useful digital twin of the assembled robot;
3. add low-speed forward walking without losing the stable marching behavior;
4. improve robustness to millimetre-scale floor unevenness and hardware variation;
5. later train a separate full command-conditioned policy for forward/backward, lateral, and yaw motion.

The first three objectives have materially succeeded. The calibrated real robot held stably, marched unsupported for a 30-second run (3 seconds hold followed by about 27 seconds marching), and subsequently walked forward using `forward_walk_v1_iter950.onnx`. The forward policy works on a truly flat floor and uses approximately 10–12 mm full-sole swing clearance. A small runtime balance trim improved a repeatable physical-left lean: +0.5 degrees `--balance-trim-right` extended one real walking trial from roughly 9.4 seconds to roughly 26.6 seconds before the safety cutoff.

The open problem is robustness. The working forward policy falls on even slightly uneven ground. A robustness fine-tune from checkpoint 950 is being trained using rough terrain, latency, friction, mass/inertia, gain, encoder, CoM, reset, and push randomization. A separate `full_env_cfg.py` has also been written but is not the policy currently being trained.

## 3. Physical robot

### 3.1 Mechanical structure

K2 has two six-DOF legs and no upper body. Per leg, the chain is:

```text
hip_pitch -> hip_roll -> hip_yaw -> knee -> ankle_pitch -> ankle_roll -> foot
```

The printed links are plain PLA. Each physical sole has or has been tested with a very thin foam sheet. This is important: policies that succeed by sliding a sole in low-friction simulation can stick on the foam in reality. The forward task therefore rewards true swing clearance and penalizes loaded-foot slip.

The robot is mechanically self-supporting in home and crouch poses because the geared servos resist back-driving. A robot appearing to “hold” with the policy enabled does not alone prove active balance; IMU response, command tracking, push recovery, and gait behavior are needed to distinguish active control from gearbox holding.

Fasteners, wiring, the Pi, and power electronics were not all explicit CAD components. The base mass was increased to include the Pi and power modules. Screws and wires were initially treated as small, but they matter through aggregate mass and, more importantly, CoM location and asymmetry. The assembled robot's measured total mass is **1.280 kg**. Individual link/component weights and a physical CoM measurement remain desirable future measurements.

### 3.2 Servos and power

- 12 × Feetech STS3215 serial-bus servos;
- nominal supply: 12 V;
- supply used: 12 V, 50 A SMPS with silicone wiring;
- Waveshare servo board with two physical output ports; six servos are connected to each port, but electrically/control-wise they form the expected serial bus configuration;
- servo capability modeled as 2.94 N·m stall torque and 4.712 rad/s no-load speed;
- encoder resolution is 4096 counts per revolution;
- goal counts are clamped to `[40, 4055]` to prevent single-turn wraparound.

The successful 30-second march reported a minimum measured bus voltage of 11.8 V at `ankle_roll_R` and no nonzero servo error samples. This does not look like a supply brownout. Temperature telemetry reported a maximum of 49 C and 121 rejected temperature outliers, while physical inspection did not indicate heating. Treat those temperature spikes as telemetry-quality issues unless independently confirmed; do not disable voltage/error monitoring.

### 3.3 IMU

The IMU is an LSM6DS3/LSM6-series device mounted at the location represented in the MJCF electronics tray. Its chip axes are not the root axes and are not the MuJoCo IMU site axes. The calibrated transformation is implemented in `hardware/k2_attitude.py` and the mounting-level correction is stored in `hardware/imu_level_calibration.json`.

The crucial measured mapping is:

```text
root_x = chip_y
root_y = -chip_x
root_z = chip_z
```

Held-forward, held-backward, held-right, stationary, and dynamic gyro-motion tests were performed. Before correction, physical forward/right tilts had inverted horizontal projected-gravity signs. After correction, the gyro/gravity consistency test achieved approximately 0.990 correlation and 1.004 gain over 369 moving samples. Stationary tilt after level calibration was approximately 0.028 degrees during the calibration test. Preserve this frame conversion; “simplifying” it back to raw chip axes will break closed-loop balance.

## 4. Coordinate, naming, and leg-mapping conventions

World/root convention:

```text
+X = robot forward
+Y = robot's own left
+Z = up
```

Fusion’s visual/body naming is misleading: MJCF bodies with `_right` are located at positive root Y, which is the physical robot’s left. The deployed mapping deliberately accounts for this. The policy/MJCF `_R` joint block drives physical servos 0–5, and `_L` drives physical servos 6–11 after the permanent mapping correction. Do not “fix” names based on appearance without redoing the matched-phase lift test.

This was a central calibration discovery. Symmetric crouches and isolated direction descriptions cannot reveal a left/right leg block swap because the checks are mirror-symmetric. The fault becomes obvious when the gait selects a support leg: before the mapping correction the physical first lift and torso shift disagreed with the twin. A diagnostic swapped one-lift then lifted the expected opposite foot. The swap was made permanent by changing the mapping and carrying each physical servo's zero/sign with it. The final mapping was validated over multiple lifts and a continuous march.

Fusion-generated joint names chain parent and child names. Consequently, `hip_pitch` is also a substring of the hip-roll joint name. Always use the anchored regular expressions in `k2_rl/k2_constants.py::JOINT_PATTERNS`; never use an unanchored expression like `.*hip_pitch.*`.

## 6. Digital twin and model construction

Primary model: `mjcf/k2_physics.xml`.

The CAD-to-twin pipeline is:

```bash
python scripts/phase1_mass.py
python scripts/phase2_make_mjcf.py
python scripts/phase3_validate.py
MUJOCO_GL=egl python scripts/render_check.py
python -c "from hardware import k2_conventions as C; C.write_joint_limits()"
```

The original Fusion export assigned steel-like material mass and was far too heavy. The pipeline replaces link masses/inertias with print/hardware estimates, adds servo masses, and scales the resulting complete model to the measured assembled mass of 1.280 kg. Until component-by-component weights or a physical suspension/balance CoM measurement are available, the model preserves the CAD-derived relative distribution and uses CoM/inertia domain randomization during training.

Important simulation facts:

- physics timestep is 0.002 s;
- control decimation is 10, giving a 50 Hz policy/control rate;
- each hinge has `armature=0.01` to represent reflected rotor inertia and prevent contact chatter;
- only foot collision geometry is active, avoiding self-snagging printed shells;
- all five reward/diagnostic sites on each sole were mirrored to within 0.001 mm;
- the feet contain center, heel, toe, inner, and outer sites used for full-sole lift/flatness/clearance metrics;
- MuJoCo is currently sufficient; Isaac Sim is available but changing simulators does not solve an unidentified parameter/calibration gap by itself.

At 5 ms physics timestep, contact injected energy and the robot bounced unrealistically; retain 2 ms unless a full stability revalidation is performed. The policy loop remains 50 Hz.

### Making the twin closer to the real robot

The twin should not be made “perfect” by tuning a single nominal model until one log matches. A useful twin consists of a measured nominal model plus bounded distributions that include plausible real hardware. The current approach is:

1. exact total mass (1.280 kg);
2. measured geometry and joint axes/limits from CAD and hardware corrections;
3. exact captured home/crouch and permanent leg mapping;
4. correct IMU frame and level offset;
5. STS3215 torque/speed, PD gains, armature, rate, count quantization, target clamping, slew behavior, and command delay;
6. foam-compatible friction and full-sole contact behavior;
7. randomization of remaining uncertain properties rather than inventing precise values.

High-value future measurements are: individual assembled segment weights, physical CoM by balancing/suspension in multiple orientations, servo step-response under representative load, foam-floor static/dynamic friction, joint backlash/deadband, and repeated synchronized current/voltage/position logs. These should update both the nominal twin and randomization bounds.

## 11. Forward-only robustness configuration

The current robustness edit deliberately preserves the successful forward gait, observations, and reward values. It changes the training distribution only.

Production training retains the original validated flat plane. Heightfields, adjacent box grids, and even a generated continuous slope all produced rare MuJoCo-Warp NaNs in long 2048-environment runs. Uneven support is therefore represented through per-environment sole-geometry randomization: the left and right sole collision geoms independently receive ±1.5 mm vertical offset and ±1 degree roll/pitch mounting error. This creates up to 3 mm unequal support and direct sole/floor angular mismatch without entering the unstable terrain-generator collision path.

Additional current randomization:

- foot friction: 0.7–1.4, shared by the two sole geoms;
- validated randomized substep action latency: 0–10 ms;
- pseudo-inertia: ±15% on massive robot links only;
- stiffness and damping scales: 0.8–1.2;
- encoder/home bias: ±0.02 rad;
- base CoM offset: x/y ±10 mm, z ±20 mm;
- moderate pushes every 3–5 s: translation and roll/pitch up to ±0.18, yaw up to ±0.25;
- reset yaw restricted to ±0.08 rad for a world-X forward/lateral-corridor task.

The base task already included the validated dynamics randomization above. Robustness training adds continuous slope variation, foam-compatible friction, and measured command latency while keeping the learned gait definition unchanged. Earlier attempts widened every dynamics range simultaneously; those combinations were removed because rare physics divergence prevented useful long training.

### Numerical-instability investigation

Three production attempts using the initially wider randomization failed before the first new checkpoint:

```text
forward_robust_rough_latency_v7     4096 envs, failed around iteration 968
forward_robust_rough_latency_v7b    2048 envs, failed around iteration 955
forward_robust_rough_latency_v7c    2048 envs, failed around iteration 960
```

Short diagnostics succeeded:

- 64 environments for 20 updates;
- 2048 environments for 20 updates (`diagnose_large`).

Therefore the config parsed, ran, and optimized, but a rare long-horizon environment diverged. MuJoCo/mjlab NaN guarding isolated environment 1059 at environment step 3085. The diagnostic files are outside the repository:

```text

/tmp/mjlab/nan_dumps/nan_dump_latest.npz
/tmp/mjlab/nan_dumps/model_20260816_071723.mjb
```

The rolling 100-step physics-state history was finite immediately before each detected NaN, implying sudden contact divergence. Tightening `-25%` inertia / `0.55x` stiffness and doubling CCD did not cure it. A box-grid run saved checkpoint 1000 then failed around 1007; a continuous-slope run failed around 1086; removing the experimental whole-step latency queue still failed around 1030. Diffing the exact successful checkpoint-950 environment snapshot showed the terrain generator was the only substantive remaining difference. Production `v14` therefore uses the validated plane plus per-environment sole height/angle randomization. It passed checkpoints 1100 and 1200 with full-length episodes and zero falls/collapses. NaN guarding remains enabled.

Do not accept a checkpoint merely because one exists: verify that training reached it without NaN, inspect reward/episode length and termination trends, then replay it on flat and rough digital-twin terrain before exporting for hardware.

## 13. Full locomotion configuration

`k2_rl/full_env_cfg.py` is a separate task registered as `Mjlab-Full-K2`. It was created by using the working forward design as a baseline, but it is not a fine-tune target for the forward checkpoint because its command and observation semantics differ.

Training commands are body-frame:

```text
vx:  -0.04 to +0.04 m/s
vy:  -0.02 to +0.02 m/s
wz:  -0.35 to +0.35 rad/s
```

Ten percent of training environments receive standing commands. Moving commands use the alternating gait clock. The actor sees the three-component twist command immediately before the gait observation. Heading is not included because yaw-rate control should remain rotation-invariant and should not depend on long-term gyro-integrated yaw.

The task removes zero-translation/zero-yaw penalties that would fight its commands, widens hip roll/yaw/ankle-roll pose tolerance, and retains full-sole lift/contact/slip/flatness shaping and the same terrain/randomization concept. It should train from scratch:

```bash
python -m k2_rl.train Mjlab-Full-K2 \
  --env.scene.num-envs 4096 \
  --agent.max-iterations 3000 \
  --agent.run-name full_v1
```

Before beginning a long full-locomotion run, copy the long-horizon stability correction from the forward config into `full_env_cfg.py`: avoid the already-observed `-25% inertia / 0.55x stiffness` corner. Then perform 20-update and at least 100-update diagnostics with NaN guarding. The full policy also needs deployment runner support for its twist-command observation before any real test; current `k2_policy_run.py` explicitly supports the 45/46/48-D march/forward layouts, not an arbitrary full-locomotion observation.

## 14. PPO and training architecture

`k2_rl/ppo_cfg.py` defines separate experiment names while sharing the PPO architecture:

- actor MLP: 256, 128, 64, ELU;
- critic MLP: 256, 128, 64, ELU;
- actor and critic observation normalization enabled;
- Gaussian action distribution, scalar initial standard deviation 1.0;
- clipped PPO value loss coefficient 1.0;
- policy clip 0.2;
- entropy coefficient 0.01;
- 5 learning epochs, 4 minibatches;
- adaptive learning rate starting at 1e-3;
- gamma 0.99, lambda 0.95;
- desired KL 0.01;
- gradient norm clamp 1.0;
- 24 steps per environment per PPO update.

## 15. Simulation validation before hardware

For every candidate robustness checkpoint:

1. replay at `0.01 m/s`, because this is the already-tested physical command;
2. replay at 0.02, 0.03, and 0.04 m/s to check the full trained range;
3. measure full-sole minimum apex clearance, sole height spread, inner-edge gap, and foot-to-foot collision contacts;
4. verify heading and lateral drift over at least 30 seconds;
5. verify it can recover from moderate pushes without exaggerated side lift;
6. verify nominal flat performance was not destroyed by roughness optimization;
7. compare action magnitude and joint speeds against checkpoint 950;
8. reject any candidate with simulation NaNs or unexplained contact explosions.

Example nominal twin command after export:

```bash
python -m hardware.k2_policy_run \
  --bus sim --viewer --mode walk --forward-speed 0.01 \
  --duration 30 --fall-angle 16 \
  --policy policies/forward_robust_iterXXXX.onnx \
  --log robust_sim_iterXXXX_001.csv
```

Because `hardware.k2_policy_run --bus sim` uses the nominal MJCF, it is not sufficient by itself to validate rough-terrain robustness. Also use the mjlab play environment/config or a controlled generated-terrain evaluation. The nominal bus replay is still valuable because it exactly exercises deployment observation and action code.

## 16. Safe real-robot evaluation protocol

Only test real hardware after a candidate passes the preceding checks. Preserve checkpoint 950 as the fallback.

1. Put the robot on the flattest surface first, with a human spotter holding close without loading the body.
2. Verify the calibrated crouch and stationary IMU tilt.
3. Run hold for a short duration.
4. Run a one-lift/very short walk with conservative cutoff.
5. Run 8–10 seconds at 0.01 m/s on flat ground.
6. Increase to 20–30 seconds only if tracking, voltage, tilt, and sound are normal.
7. Apply the known `--balance-trim-right 0.5` initially if using the same physical assembly and direction convention, but also compare an untrimmed logged run.
8. Test unevenness progressively: 1–2 mm controlled shim/texture before arbitrary floor seams. Never jump directly to aggressive rough ground.
9. Save every CSV with policy iteration, command, trim, surface, and trial number in the filename.

Template command:

```bash
cd /home/pi/K2
.venv/bin/python -m hardware.k2_policy_run \
  --bus /dev/ttyAMA0 --mode walk --forward-speed 0.01 \
  --march-after 3 --duration 10 --approach 3 \
  --slew 1.0 --fall-angle 12 \
  --balance-trim-right 0.5 \
  --policy policies/forward_robust_iterXXXX.onnx \
  --log forward_robust_iterXXXX_flat_001.csv --yes
```

If the robot consistently leans left again, do not immediately increase trim. Compare commanded/measured ankle roll, hip roll, knee tracking, stationary projected gravity, initial base pose, and per-side load/current. A consistent lean may be a CoM offset, calibration bias, compliance, foam asymmetry, or servo response mismatch. The trim is a safe small compensation, not a substitute for diagnosis.

## 17. Known risks and unresolved questions

1. **Physical CoM is not directly measured.** Total mass is measured, but distribution is estimated. CoM randomization helps but does not identify the nominal offset.
2. **Individual component weights are incomplete.** A scale capable of measuring links/components would improve inertia and CoM estimates.
3. **Servo temperature telemetry has rejected outliers.** Physical heating was not observed, but the sensor/read path deserves validation.
4. **Loaded servo bandwidth differs from simulation.** March logs show knee RMS errors around 3.7–3.8 degrees and peaks around 8.5 degrees.
5. **The current forward policy has a small physical-left bias.** Runtime trim helps. Robustness training should cover plausible lateral CoM/encoder/gain asymmetry.
6. **Rough-terrain physics can become numerically unstable under overly aggressive parameter combinations.** NaN guard is mandatory during long diagnostics.
7. **Full locomotion is only a config/smoke-tested task.** It is not trained, exported, or supported by the real runner yet.
8. **The repository documentation contains a few historical measurements.** Prefer current checked-in JSON/model values and this dated handover when numbers conflict; update older prose when committing.
9. **A mechanically held pose can mask weak active control.** Validate feedback dynamically.
10. **A symmetric pose can mask a left/right leg mapping error.** Validate with phase-selected single lifts.
